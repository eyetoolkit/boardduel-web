How to play Chess

Chess is the most studied game in the world: two armies of sixteen pieces, one board, and no hidden information. This guide covers movement, special rules and the patterns that end games.

The goal: checkmate the enemy king — attack it in a way it cannot escape. There is no clock racing you; chess is about outthinking and outmanoeuvring.

Rules

  • A chessboard has 64 squares in eight rows and eight files
  • Each side starts with 8 pawns plus king, queen, 2 rooks, 2 knights and 2 bishops
  • White moves first, then players alternate one move each
  • A piece may capture an enemy piece by moving onto its square
  • The queen sits on her own colour: white queen on a light square, black queen on a dark square

Strategy

1. Fight for the centre

Put pawns and pieces where they control the middle four squares. Pieces placed centrally reach more of the board.

2. Develop, then attack

Bring knights and bishops off the back rank before launching anything. An attack with half your pieces at home rarely works.

3. Castle early

Castling tucks the king behind pawns and connects your rooks in a single move. Do it before the middlegame gets sharp.

4. Do not bring the queen out too soon

An early queen gets chased by minor pieces, costing you tempo while your opponent develops for free.

Pro tip: before every move, check what your opponent threatens. Most games at this level are lost to a threat that was never seen, not to a clever attack.

Frequently asked

Can a pawn capture straight ahead?

No. Pawns move forward but capture only diagonally forward.

Can a knight be captured mid-jump?

No. Only the destination square matters — the knight passes over everything in between.

What if only the two kings remain?

That is a draw. Neither side can deliver checkmate with a lone king.

What is the fastest checkmate?

Scholar's mate — four moves, using queen and bishop against f7 (or f2 for black). Easy to learn, and easy to defend once you have seen it.
