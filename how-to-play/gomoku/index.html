How to Play Gomoku

Gomoku — also known as Five in a Row — is an elegant strategy game with origins in ancient China. Two players take turns placing stones on a grid, racing to connect five in a row.

The Goal: Place five of your stones in a continuous line — horizontally, vertically, or diagonally — before your opponent does. The first player to achieve five in a row wins immediately.

📖 Basic Rules

  • Played on a 15×15 grid (or larger)
  • Black (first player) and White (second player) take turns placing one stone per turn
  • Stones are placed on the intersections (not the squares)
  • Once placed, stones cannot be moved or removed
  • Win condition: Five consecutive stones in a row — horizontally, vertically, or diagonally

🧠 Winning Strategies

1. Control the Center

The center of the board has the most potential lines. Early in the game, place your stones near the center to maximize future options and limit your opponent's possibilities.

2. Build Multiple Threats

Create overlapping attack patterns. If you have two lines developing simultaneously, your opponent can only block one — forcing a win on the other.

3. Watch for Open Fours

An "open four" (four in a row with open ends on both sides) is a winning move — your opponent cannot block it without blocking the other end, which you win immediately. Always be on the lookout for opportunities to create or defend against open fours.

4. The "Overline" Trap

In some rulesets, six or more in a row also wins. Placing more than five can be a surprise tactic. In BoardDuel's rules, exactly five is the winning condition.

💡 Pro tip: The best Gomoku players think several moves ahead, imagining all possible five-in-a-row lines their opponent could complete. Before placing a stone, ask: "Does this create a winning threat? Does this block a winning threat?"

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Who goes first in Gomoku?

Black (the first player) traditionally places first. Some rule variants give white a compensation (like allowing a swap option) to balance the slight first-move advantage.

Can you win by placing more than five?

In BoardDuel, exactly five consecutive stones wins. Six or more is also a winning configuration in some rule sets.

Is Gomoku the same as Renju?

Renju is a professional variant of Gomoku with additional rules to balance the game, such as forbidden patterns for the first player. Gomoku in its standard form has no such restrictions.

What board size does BoardDuel use?

BoardDuel uses a 15×15 board, which is the standard tournament size and provides the best balance of strategy and variety.
