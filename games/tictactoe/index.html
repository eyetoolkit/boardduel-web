01 — BOARD LANGUAGE

Six states, same vocabulary, smaller canvas.

The gomoku pilot fixed the family rules: pieces carry the game, ember and teal stay UI, gold is spend once. Tic-tac-toe is the first test of those rules at nine squares — where shape replaces fill, and the frame disappears.

Your mark / their mark

X and O are drawn strokes, not glyphs — the live site renders text ✕ ◯; we render pen lines with round caps. Allegiance is carried by shape alone. Both marks are paper; no faction color, exactly like the stones.

Last move

The same ember dot from the pilot, seated at the mark's center. On a board this small one dot is plenty — no rings, no glow, no chrome.

WIN

Ghost + certainty chip

Dashed stroke = the move you're considering. The chip is tictactoe's upgrade over gomoku's hint ranks: the engine scores are exact, so the ghost can honestly say WIN, DRAW or LOSS.

The strike

Gold's single appearance: a pen line through the three, overshooting the marks the way a hand would. Gomoku rings the five; tictactoe strikes the three. Same scarcity, different noun.

04 — SCREEN 3 · END

The strike, then the truth.

The end state does two jobs: celebrate the line, and teach the game. This finish is a real engine game — Punisher as X, Counter as O — including the move where O lost it.

You win move 7 · line 3–5–7 the losing move: O→8 (move 2)
The draw gets equal billing: "1 → 5 → 2 → 3 → 7 → 4 → 6 → 8 → 9" — Punisher vs Punisher, one of 20/20 draws. All nine filled, no strike, nothing dims. The bar reads: all nine filled · nobody broke through.
The strike animates once. A gold pen-line draws across the three in half a second — the page's single choreographed moment, skipped entirely for reduced-motion users. Non-winning marks dim a step; the strike never does.
"The losing move" is the retention hook. The result bar names the exact mark where the game slipped — here O→8 on move 2, when a draw was still guaranteed. Review replays to that square with the certainty map attached.
Rematch inverts the first mark. Loser draws first; after a draw, swap. X's tree advantage is real — the lobby shouldn't pretend a coin flip is fair twice in a row.

06 — DESIGN NOTES

What this game adds to the family.

a
Shape carries allegiance. Stones used fill (black/white); marks use form (X/O). Either way, pieces stay neutral of faction color — ember and teal remain clock-and-chip furniture. Connect4 will use the same rule: two checkers distinguished by fill, never by faction color.
b
Gold is still spend once. Gomoku: five rings. Tictactoe: one strike, animated a single time. When Connect4 ships, gold is the four-in-a-row connector line. Same token, same scarcity.
c
Smaller board, bolder lines. 2px inner hash, no outer frame, 9px mark strokes. Density set the weight on the 15×15; here the slate itself is the frame and the hash floats like a doodle on it.
d
Certainty beats probability. Gomoku's hint panel showed raw weights; tictactoe's shows verdicts, because the game is solved and the engine proves it live. Same component, honest per game — and it sets the ceiling for Chess hints later.
e
The emoji layer goes. The live page greets results with emoji and renders marks as text glyphs. The redesign draws its marks and speaks its results: "You win · the losing move was O→8". Nothing to strip out later.
f
Every number is real. Tree counts (255,168 games), opening win rates (99.4% / 96.2% / 98.3%), the 400-game duel, 20/20 draws, the mid-position verdicts and the losing move — all produced by the shipped engine in this session. The draft doubles as its own regression test.

Open questions

  • Undo scope: free take-backs vs the engine, consent-based vs humans, or off in ranked?
  • Should Counter sandbag? It never sandbags today — easy won 0 of 400 against hard, and easy still beats beginners by blocking half the time. Recommendation: keep it honest.
