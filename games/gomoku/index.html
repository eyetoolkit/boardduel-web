The stones are the paper.

Across the ARENA, paper is whatever carries the truth of the game: the card face, the given digit, the cage sum, the tower brick, the target card. Here it is the stone. Black and white carry everything the game knows — ember and teal never touch them. They stay UI: clocks, last-move dots, turn chips. Gold belongs to exactly one thing — the win line.

01 — BOARD LANGUAGE

Six states. One vocabulary for all five games.

This pilot fixes the rendering rules every BoardDuel game will inherit — stone fill, ghost preview, last-move dot, win ring, coordinates, star points. Once these read instantly, Connect4 checkers, Reversi discs and Chess pieces are the same language with different nouns.

Your stone / their stone

Black = you, white = opponent. Pure fill, no faction color — allegiance is told by turn chips and clocks, never by painting the stone.

Last move

A single ember dot, centered, on whichever stone moved last. On a 225-point board this is the difference between following the game and getting lost.

Ghost / win ring

Dashed mute circle = the move you're about to make (with hint rank inside when hints are on). Gold ring = a completed five. Gold appears nowhere else.

Point / star point

Intersections, not squares — stones sit on crossings. Five star points anchor the eye: four 3-3 corners plus tengen H8.

Coordinates A–O / 1–15

Columns A–O left to right, rows 15→1 top to bottom. Standard gomoku notation, I kept. Recessive: 58% size, mute-2, recede further on phones.

Notation rail

Moves listed in pairs, tabular numerals, like a flight recorder. Any move is a review anchor — tap one and the board rewinds to it (proposed).

Five in a row, gold, done.

This win is the argument for the honesty section below. A greedy near-stone player — zero lookahead, just "place where my count is highest" — beat the engine's hardest level in 35 moves. D6 – E6 – F6 – G6 – H6 is a real engine output, not a staged position.

Draws exist and get a screen. Our simulation also produced a full 225-point fill with no winner. Same bar, neutral paper text: "Board full · nobody broke through".
05 — WHAT THE SERVER ACTUALLY DOES

Design against the real engine, not the brochure.

Three facts verified in the shipped code. Each one changes what this UI is allowed to promise.

P1 · false advertising

The forbidden-move toggle doesn't exist

Three meta tags advertise "switchable global forbidden-move rules". No implementation ships. The draft's lobby never shows a rules toggle; mode copy says freestyle explicitly.

Until the engine supports renju checks, hiding the claim costs nothing and removes the only place the site currently overstates itself.

P1 · one-char bug

The AI opens in the corner — by sign error

The comment promises center-weighted openings. The code adds distance-from-center instead of subtracting it, so the engine's first move drifts to B14. Ten self-play moves, all hugging one corner:

B14 C14 B13 B12 C13 D13 D12 E11 E12 F12

Flip + (center * 3) to - (center * 3). The mini board above re-renders honestly different the day it ships.

P1 · strength theater

"Hard" lost to a greedy human

All three AI levels are weighted pattern counts — no search, no lookahead. In the proof game on this page, a player who only maximizes their own local count beat "hard" in 35 moves.

Lobby copy names levels by behavior (Counter / Attacker / Punisher), and the real fix is the queued P1: shallow search rework. Until then, no ranking claims on the engine path.

06 — DESIGN NOTES

What this pilot locks in.

a
Stones carry the game. Ember and teal never paint game pieces on any BoardDuel board — they stay on clocks, dots and turn chips. This is the family rule Connect4 and Reversi will inherit unchanged.
b
Gold is spend once. Win ring and result bar, nothing else. When Chess ships, gold means checkmate — the same scarcity keeps it loud.
c
Slate, not wood. A wooden board would drag the ARENA back to warm-brown defaults. Dark slate + stone contrast does the work; the grid lines are the same line token as every other draft.
d
Coordinates recede. A–O / 15–1 ship at 58% size in mute-2 and shrink further under 620px. They're for review and chat notation, never for decoration.
e
Every position is engine output. Hero, match, hint weights, both wins and the corner cluster come from the live ai.js. Zero staged boards — the draft is a regression test it can wear.
f
Two-step placing on touch. Tap shows the ghost, tap again confirms. Mis-taps on a 225-point grid are the #1 mobile rage-quit; desktop keeps hover-ghost + click.

Open questions

  • Hint economy: free raw weights in casual, coin-priced "one bar only" in ranked? The engine computes both for free either way.
  • Undo scope: one free undo everywhere, or casual-only? Ranked rooms already have an opponent to veto it.
  • Coordinates: keep standard I column, or skip to J like some chess sets do? Standard keeps notation compatible with every gomoku tool.
