How to Play Chess

Chess is the world's most studied game — a battle of wits between two armies, each with 16 pieces. This guide covers everything from piece movement to checkmate patterns.

The Goal: Checkmate your opponent's king — meaning the king is under attack and cannot escape. Unlike most games, there's no timer ticking down to defeat. Chess is about outthinking, outmaneuvering, and outlasting your opponent.

♟️ The Chess Board and Setup

A chessboard has 64 squares (8×8), alternating light and dark. Each player starts with:

  • 8 Pawns — Row 2 (white) / Row 7 (black)
  • 8 Pieces: 1 King, 1 Queen, 2 Rooks, 2 Knights, 2 Bishops
  • Queens on their own color — White queen on a white square, black queen on a black square

🎯 How Each Piece Moves

♔

King

One square in any direction: horizontal, vertical, or diagonal. The most important piece — lose it and you lose the game.

♕

Queen

The most powerful piece. Moves any number of squares in any direction: horizontal, vertical, or diagonal.

♖

Rook

Any number of squares horizontally or vertically. Excellent for controlling open files and the back rank.

♗

Bishop

Any number of squares diagonally. Each bishop stays on its starting color for the entire game.

♘

Knight

Moves in an "L" shape: 2 squares in one direction, then 1 square perpendicular. The only piece that can jump over others.

♙

Pawn

Forward 1 square (2 from starting position). Captures diagonally forward. Can promote to any piece upon reaching the last rank.

⭐ Special Moves

Castling

King moves 2 squares toward a rook, and the rook jumps over the king to the adjacent square. Conditions: neither piece has moved, king is not in check, king doesn't pass through an attacked square.

En Passant

If a pawn moves 2 squares from its starting position and lands beside an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn can capture it as if it only moved 1 square. This capture must happen on the very next move.

Pawn Promotion

When a pawn reaches the opponent's back rank, it must be promoted to a Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight. In most cases, promoting to a Queen is best.

⚔️ Key Concepts

Check

When your king is under attack. You must resolve check on your next move — by capturing the attacking piece, moving the king, or blocking the attack.

Checkmate

When the king is in check and has no legal move to escape. The game ends immediately. The player delivering checkmate wins.

Opening Principles

1. Control the center early with pawns and pieces.
2. Develop your knights and bishops (get them off the back rank).
3. Castle early to protect your king.
4. Don't bring your queen out too early — she can be chased by minor pieces.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can a pawn capture a piece directly in front of it?

No. Pawns can only capture diagonally forward, not straight ahead. They move forward but capture diagonally.

Can a knight be captured while jumping?

No. The knight is the only piece that jumps over other pieces. It cannot be captured mid-jump — only the destination square matters.

What happens if only kings remain?

The game is a draw (stalemate). No side can deliver checkmate with just a king. Stalemate occurs when one player has no legal moves but is not in check.

How long does a chess game take?

It varies enormously. Casual games might last 15-30 minutes. Tournament games use time controls (e.g., 15+10: each player gets 15 minutes plus 10 seconds per move).

What's the Scholar's Mate?

The fastest checkmate in chess — only 4 moves. It involves queen and bishop delivering a quick checkmate on f7 (or f2 for black). Easy to learn, fun to surprise opponents with!
